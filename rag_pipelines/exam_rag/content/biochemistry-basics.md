Biochemistry studies the chemical processes that occur inside living
organisms — how molecules like carbohydrates, proteins, lipids, and
nucleic acids are built, broken down, and used for energy and structure.

**The four major biomolecules:**
- **Carbohydrates** (sugars, starch, cellulose) — built from simple sugar
  units (monosaccharides like glucose). Their main role is energy storage
  and supply; cellulose also provides structural support in plant cell
  walls.
- **Proteins** — chains of amino acids folded into specific 3D shapes.
  Function depends entirely on shape: enzymes (biological catalysts),
  structural proteins (keratin, collagen), transport proteins
  (hemoglobin), and antibodies are all proteins.
- **Lipids** (fats, oils, phospholipids) — energy storage (more
  energy-dense than carbohydrates) and, as phospholipids, the main
  component of all cell membranes.
- **Nucleic acids** (DNA, RNA) — store and transmit genetic information.
  DNA is a double helix of nucleotide base pairs (A-T, G-C); RNA is
  single-stranded and carries out DNA's instructions.

**Enzymes** are protein catalysts that speed up biochemical reactions
without being consumed themselves, by lowering the activation energy
needed. Each enzyme has an **active site** shaped to fit a specific
substrate (the "lock and key" model) — this is why enzymes are highly
specific and why a change in temperature or pH that distorts the
enzyme's shape (denaturation) destroys its function.

**ATP (adenosine triphosphate)** is the cell's immediate energy currency.
Energy released from breaking down glucose (via respiration) is used to
attach a third phosphate group to ADP, forming ATP; breaking that bond
back off releases usable energy for cellular work.
