Mechanics is the branch of physics that studies motion and the forces that
cause it. A few core ideas cover most introductory exam questions:

**Newton's three laws of motion:**
1. **First law (inertia)**: an object at rest stays at rest, and an object
   in motion stays in motion at constant velocity, unless acted on by a net
   external force.
2. **Second law**: force equals mass times acceleration — F = ma. This is
   the single most-used equation in mechanics; it tells you that the same
   force produces more acceleration on a lighter object than a heavier one.
3. **Third law**: for every action force, there is an equal and opposite
   reaction force — when you push on a wall, the wall pushes back on you
   with equal force.

**Kinematics (describing motion without worrying about its cause)** uses
these core equations, assuming constant acceleration:
- v = u + at (final velocity = initial velocity + acceleration × time)
- s = ut + ½at² (displacement)
- v² = u² + 2as (relates velocity and displacement directly, without time)

**Energy** comes in two main mechanical forms: kinetic energy
(KE = ½mv², the energy of motion) and potential energy (commonly
gravitational PE = mgh, the energy stored due to height). In a closed
system with no friction, total mechanical energy (KE + PE) is conserved —
energy converts between the two forms but the total stays constant.

**Worked example:** A 2 kg object starts at rest and accelerates at 3 m/s²
for 4 seconds. Find its final velocity and the distance traveled.
v = u + at = 0 + (3)(4) = 12 m/s.
s = ut + ½at² = 0 + ½(3)(16) = 24 m.

**Worked example 2:** What force is needed to accelerate a 5 kg object at
2 m/s²?
F = ma = 5 × 2 = 10 N.
