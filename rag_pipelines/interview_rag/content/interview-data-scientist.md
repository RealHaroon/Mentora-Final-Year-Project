**Behavioral:**
- "Tell me about a time you worked with messy or incomplete data. What did
  you do?" — a strong answer covers: how the data's problems were
  identified (missing values, inconsistent formats, outliers), the
  specific cleaning/imputation decisions made, and how those decisions
  were justified rather than arbitrary.
- "Describe a time your analysis led to a business decision you disagreed
  with, or vice versa." — a strong answer covers: how the disagreement was
  resolved through evidence/communication rather than just deferring or
  digging in, and what the actual outcome was.

**Technical:**
- "Explain the bias-variance tradeoff." — a strong answer covers: high
  bias means the model is too simple and underfits (misses real patterns);
  high variance means the model is too sensitive to the specific training
  data and overfits (doesn't generalize); the goal is balancing the two,
  typically via model complexity, regularization, or more training data.
- "How would you evaluate a classification model if the classes are
  imbalanced?" — a strong answer covers: why plain accuracy is misleading
  on imbalanced data, and mentions precision, recall, F1-score, or
  ROC-AUC as more informative alternatives, with reasoning for which
  metric matters most given the specific cost of false positives vs.
  false negatives in context.
- "Walk me through how you'd approach a new dataset you've never seen
  before." — a strong answer covers: exploratory data analysis first
  (distributions, missing values, correlations) before any modeling,
  understanding what each column actually represents, and checking for
  data leakage or label errors before trusting any result.
