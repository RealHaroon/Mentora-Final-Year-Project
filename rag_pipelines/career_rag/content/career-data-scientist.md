A Data Scientist analyzes large, often messy datasets to find patterns,
build predictive models, and turn raw data into decisions a business can
act on. The role sits between statistics, programming, and domain
knowledge — a good data scientist doesn't just build an accurate model,
they can explain what it means and why it matters to people who aren't
technical.

**Typical required skills:** Python (the dominant language for this
field), statistics and probability, machine learning fundamentals
(regression, classification, clustering), SQL for querying data, and a
deep learning framework like TensorFlow or PyTorch for more advanced
modeling work. Strong communication skills matter more than most people
expect — a model's value is often limited by how well its results get
explained to stakeholders.

**Typical entry path:** a bachelor's degree in computer science,
statistics, mathematics, or a related quantitative field, often followed
by self-directed learning (online courses, Kaggle competitions, personal
projects) to build the specific ML/stats skill set, since most degree
programs don't cover the full modern data science toolkit. Many data
scientists start in an adjacent role — data analyst, business analyst, or
software engineer — and transition in as their statistics/ML skills grow.

**A realistic first 6 months on the job** usually involves more data
cleaning and exploratory analysis than modeling — real-world datasets are
messy, and a large fraction of the actual work is making sure the data is
trustworthy before any model gets built on top of it.
