career: "Financial Analyst"

A Financial Analyst evaluates an organization's financial data — past
performance, budgets, forecasts, investment options — to help leadership
make informed decisions. Depending on the industry, this can mean
corporate budgeting and forecasting, equity research, or evaluating
potential investments; the common thread is turning financial data into
a clear recommendation.

**Typical required skills:** advanced Excel (financial modeling is still
built and reviewed in spreadsheets at most organizations, far more than
newcomers expect), financial modeling (projecting future performance
from historical data and assumptions), solid accounting fundamentals
(reading and understanding financial statements), valuation methods (for
roles touching investments or M&A), data analysis more broadly, and clear
communication — a financial recommendation is only useful if the
reasoning behind it can be explained to non-finance decision-makers.

**Typical entry path:** a finance, accounting, economics, or business
degree is the most common route, often combined with a recognized
credential (CFA is the most respected for investment-focused roles) that
signals structured financial knowledge. Internships in finance or
accounting during university are a strong, common way to build the real
spreadsheet/modeling fluency that coursework alone usually doesn't cover.

**A realistic first 6 months on the job** involves a lot of time
building and maintaining models and reports for senior analysts to review
before being trusted to make recommendations independently — accuracy and
attention to detail matter more early on than original financial
insight.
