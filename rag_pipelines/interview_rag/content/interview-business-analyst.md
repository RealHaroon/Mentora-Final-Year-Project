**Behavioral:**
- "Tell me about a time you had to explain a technical or data-driven
  finding to a non-technical stakeholder." — a strong answer covers: how
  the explanation was adapted for the audience (less jargon, visual
  framing, focusing on the implication rather than the method), and how
  the stakeholder's understanding was confirmed rather than assumed.
- "Describe a time you gathered requirements from a stakeholder and they
  turned out to be incomplete or wrong." — a strong answer covers: how the
  gap was discovered, and what process change (follow-up questions,
  confirming requirements in writing) was adopted to avoid the same issue
  next time.

**Technical:**
- "How would you investigate a sudden drop in a key business metric?" —
  a strong answer covers: segmenting the drop (is it across the board or
  concentrated in one segment/region/product?), checking for a data
  pipeline or tracking issue before assuming a real business cause, and
  forming a specific testable hypothesis rather than guessing broadly.
- "What's the difference between correlation and causation, and why does
  it matter for business recommendations?" — a strong answer covers: two
  variables moving together doesn't mean one causes the other (a third
  factor could explain both), and recommending an action based on
  correlation alone risks a wrong or wasted intervention.
- "Walk me through how you'd build a dashboard for a sales team." — a
  strong answer covers: starting from what decisions the dashboard needs
  to support (not just what data is available), and prioritizing a small
  number of clear, actionable metrics over cramming in everything
  possible.
