career: "UX/UI Designer"

A UX/UI Designer decides how a digital product looks and, more
importantly, how it actually works for the person using it — researching
real user problems, structuring an interface around them, and testing
whether the result is genuinely usable before engineers build it. UX
(user experience) and UI (visual interface) are often treated as one
role early in a career, splitting into separate specialties only at more
senior levels.

**Typical required skills:** a design tool like Figma for wireframing and
high-fidelity mockups, user research methods (interviews, usability
testing, surveys) to find out what users actually need rather than
guessing, prototyping to test ideas before any code is written, an
understanding of design systems (reusable components and consistent
visual language across a product), and clear communication — a designer
has to justify design decisions to engineers and stakeholders who often
don't share the same design vocabulary.

**Typical entry path:** a portfolio of real design work matters far more
than a specific degree — many designers come from graphic design, HCI
(human-computer interaction), psychology, or are self-taught through
bootcamps and personal projects. A strong portfolio showing the actual
problem-solving process (research → iteration → final design), not just
polished final screens, is what most hiring managers look for first.

**A realistic first 6 months on the job** usually involves far more
collaboration and constraint-navigating than solo creative work — working
within an existing design system, negotiating trade-offs with engineers
about what's feasible to build, and revising designs repeatedly based on
user testing feedback rather than shipping a first design untouched.
