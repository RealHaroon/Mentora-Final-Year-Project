Genetics is the study of how traits are inherited from parents to
offspring, through units of heredity called genes, located on DNA.

**Core vocabulary:**
- **Gene**: a segment of DNA that codes for a specific trait or protein.
- **Allele**: an alternative version of a gene (e.g. the gene for eye
  color has alleles for brown, blue, etc.).
- **Genotype**: the actual allele combination an organism has (e.g. Bb).
- **Phenotype**: the observable trait that results (e.g. brown eyes).
- **Dominant allele** (capital letter, e.g. B): expressed in the
  phenotype even if only one copy is present (heterozygous, Bb).
- **Recessive allele** (lowercase, e.g. b): only expressed in the
  phenotype when two copies are present (homozygous recessive, bb).

**Mendelian inheritance.** Gregor Mendel's pea plant experiments
established that traits are inherited as discrete units (not blended),
and that each parent contributes one allele per gene. A **Punnett square**
predicts offspring genotype ratios from a cross: crossing two
heterozygotes (Bb × Bb) gives a 1:2:1 genotype ratio (BB : Bb : bb) and,
since B is dominant, a 3:1 phenotype ratio.

**DNA structure and replication.** DNA is a double helix of two
complementary strands held together by base pairing: Adenine pairs with
Thymine, Guanine pairs with Cytosine. During replication, the strands
separate and each serves as a template for building a new complementary
strand — this is why DNA replication is called "semi-conservative" (each
new DNA molecule has one original strand and one new strand).

**Mutations** are changes in the DNA sequence. Most are neutral or
harmful, but some introduce genetic variation that natural selection can
act on — the raw material for evolution.
