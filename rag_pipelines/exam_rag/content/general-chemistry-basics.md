Matter is made of atoms, and understanding atomic structure explains most
of chemistry's core patterns.

**Atomic structure:** an atom has a nucleus (containing positively charged
protons and neutral neutrons) surrounded by negatively charged electrons
in shells/orbitals. The **atomic number** is the number of protons, which
defines which element it is. The **mass number** is protons + neutrons.
Atoms of the same element with different neutron counts are **isotopes**.

**The periodic table** organizes elements by increasing atomic number into
rows (periods) and columns (groups). Elements in the same group share
similar chemical properties because they have the same number of
**valence electrons** (electrons in the outermost shell) — these are the
electrons involved in forming bonds.

**Chemical bonding** happens because atoms tend toward a stable
(typically full) outer electron shell:
- **Ionic bonds** form when one atom transfers electrons to another,
  creating oppositely charged ions that attract each other (e.g. NaCl —
  sodium loses an electron, chlorine gains it).
- **Covalent bonds** form when atoms share electrons (e.g. H₂O — oxygen
  shares electrons with two hydrogen atoms).

**Moles and stoichiometry:** a mole is a fixed count of particles
(6.022 × 10²³, Avogadro's number) — it lets chemists convert between a
measurable mass and a countable number of atoms/molecules. Molar mass
(grams per mole) is found by summing the atomic masses of all atoms in a
molecule's formula.

**Worked example:** How many moles are in 36 g of water (H₂O, molar mass
≈ 18 g/mol)?
moles = mass / molar mass = 36 / 18 = 2 moles.
