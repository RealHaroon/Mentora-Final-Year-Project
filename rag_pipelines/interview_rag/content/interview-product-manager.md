**Behavioral:**
- "Tell me about a time you had to say no to a feature request from a
  senior stakeholder." — a strong answer covers: how the decision was
  justified with evidence (user data, strategic priorities) rather than
  just personal preference, and how the relationship was maintained
  despite the disagreement.
- "Describe a product launch that didn't go as planned." — a strong
  answer covers: what specifically went wrong, what was learned, and what
  changed in how later launches were planned as a result.

**Technical / case-style:**
- "How would you prioritize a list of ten feature requests with limited
  engineering time?" — a strong answer covers: a concrete framework
  (e.g. weighing user impact against implementation effort) rather than
  just gut feeling, and acknowledging that prioritization also requires
  saying no to good ideas, not just ranking them.
- "How would you measure whether a new feature is successful after
  launch?" — a strong answer covers: defining the success metric *before*
  launch (not retrofitting one after seeing the data), tying it to the
  original goal the feature was meant to achieve, and distinguishing a
  real improvement from normal week-to-week noise in the metric.
- "A key metric dropped 20% last week — what do you do?" — a strong
  answer covers: checking for a tracking/data issue first, segmenting the
  drop to see if it's isolated to one user group or platform, and forming
  a specific hypothesis to investigate rather than reacting with an
  immediate large change.
