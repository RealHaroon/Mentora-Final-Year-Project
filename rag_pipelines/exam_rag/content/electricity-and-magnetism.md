Electricity and magnetism describe how electric charges interact, flow,
and generate forces — and how the two phenomena are deeply linked.

**Current electricity.** Electric current (I) is the rate of flow of
charge, measured in amperes. **Ohm's Law** relates voltage, current, and
resistance: V = IR, where V is the potential difference (volts) driving
the current, and R is the resistance (ohms) opposing it.

- **Series circuits**: components share the same current, but voltage
  splits across them; total resistance = R₁ + R₂ + ...
- **Parallel circuits**: components share the same voltage, but current
  splits between branches; total resistance is found from
  1/R_total = 1/R₁ + 1/R₂ + ...

**Electric power** dissipated by a component is P = VI = I²R = V²/R,
measured in watts.

**Magnetism.** A moving electric charge creates a magnetic field —
this is the basis of an **electromagnet** (current through a coiled wire
around an iron core). Conversely, a changing magnetic field near a
conductor induces an electric current — this is **electromagnetic
induction**, the principle behind electric generators.

**Key rules:**
- **Right-hand rule**: curl the right hand's fingers in the direction of
  conventional current flow around a wire; the thumb points in the
  direction of the resulting magnetic field.
- **Fleming's left-hand rule**: for a current-carrying conductor in a
  magnetic field, the thumb (force), first finger (field), and second
  finger (current) are mutually perpendicular — this gives the direction
  of the force on the conductor, the basis of an electric motor.

Like magnetic poles repel, unlike poles attract — and magnetic field
lines always run from the north pole to the south pole outside a magnet.
