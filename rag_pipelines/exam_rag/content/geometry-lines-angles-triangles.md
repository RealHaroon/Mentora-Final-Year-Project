Lines and angles are the foundation of geometry. When two lines cross, they
form angles that follow a few fixed relationships worth memorizing, because
they show up constantly in exam problems:

- **Complementary angles** add up to 90°.
- **Supplementary angles** add up to 180°.
- **Vertically opposite angles** (formed when two lines cross) are always
  equal to each other.
- **Angles on a straight line** always add up to 180°.
- When a line crosses two **parallel lines**, corresponding angles are
  equal, and alternate angles are equal — this is the basis for most
  "find the missing angle" problems involving parallel lines.

**Triangles** have their own fixed rule: the three interior angles of any
triangle always sum to exactly 180°. This single fact is usually enough to
find a missing angle if the other two are known. Triangles are also
classified by their sides and angles:

- **Equilateral**: all three sides equal, all three angles 60°.
- **Isosceles**: exactly two sides equal, and the two angles opposite those
  sides are also equal to each other.
- **Scalene**: no sides equal, no angles equal.
- **Right-angled**: one angle is exactly 90°; the side opposite it (the
  longest side) is the hypotenuse, and the Pythagorean theorem applies:
  (hypotenuse)² = (side 1)² + (side 2)².

**Worked example:** A triangle has angles of 50° and 70°. Find the third
angle.
Since all three angles sum to 180°: third angle = 180 − 50 − 70 = 60°.

**Worked example 2:** A right triangle has legs of length 3 and 4. Find the
hypotenuse.
Using the Pythagorean theorem: hypotenuse² = 3² + 4² = 9 + 16 = 25, so the
hypotenuse = 5.
