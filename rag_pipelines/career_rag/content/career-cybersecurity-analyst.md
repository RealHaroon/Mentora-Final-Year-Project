A Cybersecurity Analyst monitors an organization's systems and networks
for threats, investigates suspicious activity, and helps close the gaps
attackers could exploit. The role is defensive by default — most of the
job is watching, detecting, and responding, not "hacking back" — though
some analysts specialize into offensive security (penetration testing)
later on.

**Typical required skills:** solid networking fundamentals (how traffic
actually moves across a network, since most attacks and their detection
happen at this level), comfort with Linux, familiarity with SIEM
(Security Information and Event Management) tools used to monitor and
correlate logs across an organization, threat analysis (recognizing what
a given log entry or alert actually indicates), basic penetration-testing
concepts, and a solid grounding in core security fundamentals
(authentication, encryption, common attack types).

**Typical entry path:** a computer science or IT background combined
with a recognized security certification (such as CompTIA Security+ or,
for more advanced roles, CEH or OSCP) is a common, credible way in —
certifications carry more direct weight in this field than in most other
tech roles, since they signal verified, structured knowledge rather than
just self-reported skill. Capture-the-flag (CTF) competitions are a
widely respected way to demonstrate hands-on skill before any job
experience.

**A realistic first 6 months on the job** involves far more log review,
alert triage, and documentation than the "incident response" dramatized
in media — most days are spent distinguishing real threats from the
large volume of false positives any monitoring system generates.
