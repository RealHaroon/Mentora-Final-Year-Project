Inorganic chemistry studies the structure and reactions of compounds that
are generally not based on carbon-hydrogen chains (unlike organic
chemistry) — including elements, metals, salts, acids, and bases.

**The periodic table** organizes all known elements by increasing atomic
number into rows (periods) and columns (groups). Elements in the same
group share similar chemical properties because they have the same
number of electrons in their outermost (valence) shell — this valence
electron count is what mainly drives an element's reactivity and bonding.

**Types of chemical bonds:**
- **Ionic bonding**: a metal transfers electrons to a non-metal, forming
  oppositely charged ions that attract each other (e.g. Na⁺ and Cl⁻ in
  table salt). Ionic compounds typically have high melting points and
  conduct electricity when molten or dissolved.
- **Covalent bonding**: atoms share electron pairs (e.g. two hydrogen
  atoms sharing electrons to form H₂). Usually between non-metals.
- **Metallic bonding**: metal atoms share a "sea" of delocalized
  electrons, explaining why metals conduct electricity and heat well and
  are malleable.

**Acids and bases.** An acid donates H⁺ ions in solution (pH < 7); a base
accepts H⁺ ions or donates OH⁻ ions (pH > 7). Water is neutral (pH 7). A
**neutralization reaction** between an acid and a base produces a salt
and water: acid + base → salt + water.

**Periodic trends**: atomic radius decreases across a period (more
protons pull electrons in tighter) and increases down a group (more
electron shells); reactivity of metals increases down a group (easier to
lose outer electrons), while reactivity of non-metals increases up a
group (easier to gain electrons).
