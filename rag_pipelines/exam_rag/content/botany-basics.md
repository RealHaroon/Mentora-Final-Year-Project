Botany is the branch of biology that studies plants — their structure,
growth, reproduction, and how they convert light into usable energy.

**Plant organ systems:**
- **Roots** — anchor the plant and absorb water and minerals from soil
  via root hairs, which greatly increase surface area for absorption.
- **Stem** — supports the plant above ground and transports substances
  between roots and leaves through two specialized tissues: **xylem**
  (carries water and minerals upward, one-way, made of dead cells) and
  **phloem** (carries sugars made in the leaves to the rest of the plant,
  both directions, made of living cells).
- **Leaves** — the primary site of photosynthesis. Their flat, broad
  shape maximizes light absorption; tiny pores called **stomata** (each
  controlled by a pair of guard cells) regulate gas exchange and water
  loss.

**Photosynthesis** converts light energy, water, and carbon dioxide into
glucose and oxygen: 6CO₂ + 6H₂O + light energy → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂. It
happens in the **chloroplasts**, specifically in the green pigment
**chlorophyll**, which absorbs light (mainly red and blue wavelengths,
reflecting green — which is why plants look green).

**Plant reproduction** can be sexual (via flowers: pollen from the anther
fertilizes an ovule in the ovary, producing a seed) or asexual (e.g.
runners, tubers, cuttings — producing genetically identical offspring
without fertilization). Seed dispersal (by wind, water, or animals)
reduces competition between a parent plant and its offspring for light,
water, and nutrients.
