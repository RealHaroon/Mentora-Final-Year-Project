career: "DevOps Engineer"

A DevOps Engineer builds and maintains the infrastructure and automation
that let other engineers ship software quickly and reliably — the
pipelines that test and deploy code, the servers and cloud resources an
application runs on, and the monitoring that catches problems before
users do. The role sits at the intersection of software engineering and
systems administration.

**Typical required skills:** comfort with Linux and the command line,
Docker for packaging applications consistently across environments,
Kubernetes for running and scaling containerized applications, CI/CD
pipelines (automating build, test, and deployment steps so releases don't
depend on manual, error-prone steps), familiarity with a major cloud
provider (AWS, Azure, or GCP), and scripting (Python or Bash) to automate
the inevitable repetitive operational work.

**Typical entry path:** most DevOps engineers start as a general software
or systems engineer and move into this specialty once they're comfortable
with infrastructure and automation — it's rarely a true "entry-level"
first job. Home-lab projects (standing up a personal Kubernetes cluster,
building a CI/CD pipeline for a side project) are a common, visible way to
demonstrate these skills before having them on a résumé.

**A realistic first 6 months on the job** involves a lot of reading and
adapting to an existing, often messy, real-world infrastructure setup
before making meaningful changes to it — a surprising amount of the job
is understanding systems other people built, not designing everything
from a blank slate.
