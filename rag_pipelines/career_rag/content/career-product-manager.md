A Product Manager defines what a product should do and why, then leads
the cross-functional effort (engineering, design, marketing) needed to
actually ship it. The role doesn't typically involve writing production
code or doing the design work directly — it's about deciding priorities,
making tradeoffs between competing goals, and keeping a team aligned on
what matters most right now.

**Typical required skills:** strategic thinking (deciding what to build
and why, based on user needs and business goals), leadership and
influence without direct authority (a PM rarely manages the engineers and
designers they work with), strong communication, basic UX sensibility
(understanding what makes a product usable, even without doing the design
work personally), and roadmapping — breaking a long-term vision into a
sequence of achievable, prioritized steps.

**Typical entry path:** there's no single standard path — product
managers come from engineering, design, business, consulting, and
customer-facing roles. What tends to matter most is demonstrated ability
to identify a real user problem, make a case for solving it, and work
with a team to actually ship a solution — often shown through a past
project, internship, or even a well-documented side project rather than a
specific degree.

**What day-to-day work actually looks like:** far more time is spent in
meetings, writing specs, and talking to users or stakeholders than most
newcomers expect — the job is fundamentally about communication and
decision-making under uncertainty, not building things directly.
