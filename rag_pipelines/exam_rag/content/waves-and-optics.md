topic: "Waves & Optics"
type: "topic_explainer"
---

Waves transfer energy from one place to another without transferring
matter itself. Optics is the study of light specifically — one type of
wave — and how it reflects, refracts, and forms images.

**Wave basics:**
- **Amplitude**: the maximum displacement from the rest position —
  relates to the energy/intensity the wave carries.
- **Wavelength (λ)**: the distance between two consecutive identical
  points on a wave (e.g. crest to crest).
- **Frequency (f)**: the number of complete waves passing a point per
  second, measured in hertz (Hz).
- **Wave speed**: v = fλ — speed equals frequency times wavelength.

**Transverse vs. longitudinal waves.** In a **transverse** wave (e.g.
light, water waves), particles vibrate perpendicular to the direction
the wave travels. In a **longitudinal** wave (e.g. sound), particles
vibrate parallel to (along) the direction of travel, creating
compressions and rarefactions.

**Reflection**: when a wave bounces off a surface, the angle of
incidence equals the angle of reflection (both measured from the normal
— an imaginary line perpendicular to the surface).

**Refraction**: when light passes from one medium into another (e.g. air
into glass) at an angle, it changes speed and bends. Light bends toward
the normal when entering a denser medium (slowing down), and away from
the normal when entering a less dense medium (speeding up). This is why
a straw looks "bent" at the water's surface.

**Lenses.** A **convex (converging) lens** bulges outward and bends
parallel light rays inward to a single focal point — used to correct
long-sightedness and in magnifying glasses. A **concave (diverging)
lens** curves inward and spreads light rays apart — used to correct
short-sightedness.

**The electromagnetic spectrum** (radio, microwave, infrared, visible
light, ultraviolet, X-ray, gamma) is all the same type of wave
(oscillating electric and magnetic fields) differing only in wavelength
and frequency — all travel at the speed of light in a vacuum.
