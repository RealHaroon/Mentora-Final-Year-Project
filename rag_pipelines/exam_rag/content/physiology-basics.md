topic: "Physiology"

---

Physiology is the study of how living organisms' body systems and organs
actually function — the mechanics and chemistry behind staying alive,
not just the anatomy (structure) itself.

**The circulatory system** pumps blood around the body via the heart, a
muscular pump with four chambers (two atria, two ventricles). Blood
travels: body → right atrium → right ventricle → lungs (to pick up
oxygen, release CO₂) → left atrium → left ventricle → body. Arteries
carry blood away from the heart (usually oxygenated, thick elastic
walls to handle high pressure); veins carry blood back to the heart
(usually deoxygenated, with valves to prevent backflow); capillaries are
single-cell-thick vessels where actual gas and nutrient exchange with
tissues happens.

**The respiratory system** exchanges oxygen and carbon dioxide between
air and blood. Air travels through the trachea into bronchi, then
bronchioles, ending in tiny air sacs called **alveoli** — their huge
combined surface area, thin walls, and rich capillary network maximize
the rate of gas diffusion into and out of the blood.

**The digestive system** breaks food down mechanically and chemically
into small, absorbable molecules. Enzymes specific to each stage
(amylase for starch in the mouth, pepsin for protein in the stomach,
lipase for fats in the small intestine) each work best at a specific pH.
Most nutrient absorption happens in the **small intestine**, whose
inner wall is covered in finger-like **villi** that massively increase
surface area for absorption.

**Homeostasis** is the body's maintenance of a stable internal
environment (temperature, blood glucose, water balance) despite external
changes, typically via **negative feedback**: a change is detected, and
the body responds in the opposite direction to restore the normal range
— e.g. sweating and shivering to regulate body temperature.
