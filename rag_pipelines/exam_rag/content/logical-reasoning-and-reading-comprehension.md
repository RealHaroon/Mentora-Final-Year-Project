---
topic: "General Logical Reasoning"
type: "topic_explainer"
---

Logical reasoning and reading comprehension questions test how well you can
extract and manipulate information that's given directly in a passage or
statement — they're not testing outside knowledge, so the answer is always
derivable from what's actually written.

**Reading comprehension strategy:**
1. Read the passage once for overall meaning before looking at the
   questions — trying to answer while skimming for keywords usually causes
   you to miss the passage's actual argument or sequence of events.
2. For each question, go back to the specific part of the passage it's
   asking about rather than relying on memory of the whole passage.
3. Watch for "all of the following EXCEPT" or "which is NOT stated"
   questions — these invert the usual task, so confirm each option against
   the text rather than picking the option that merely sounds correct.
4. Distinguish between what the passage **states directly** and what can
   only be **inferred** — inference questions ask what the passage implies,
   not what it says word-for-word.

**Logical reasoning strategy:**
- **Syllogisms** (e.g. "All A are B. All B are C. Therefore, all A are C.")
  are valid only if the conclusion necessarily follows from the premises —
  test this by checking whether you can imagine a scenario where the
  premises are true but the conclusion is false; if you can, the syllogism
  is invalid.
- **Cause-and-effect** questions ask you to identify what explains an
  observed result — the correct answer must actually account for the
  specific detail in the question, not just be generally related to the
  topic.
- **Sequence/pattern** questions (number series, letter series) usually
  follow one consistent rule (add a fixed amount, alternate two operations,
  etc.) — test your guessed rule against at least three consecutive terms
  before committing to an answer.

**Worked example:** "All doctors are professionals. Some professionals are
wealthy. Therefore, some doctors are wealthy." This conclusion is **not**
valid — the premises don't guarantee that the wealthy professionals
include any doctors specifically.
