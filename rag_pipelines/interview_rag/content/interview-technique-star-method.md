The **STAR method** is the standard way to structure an answer to a
behavioral interview question (any question starting with "Tell me about
a time..." or "Describe a situation where..."). It stands for:

- **Situation** — briefly set the context: what was the project, team, or
  problem? Keep this short — one or two sentences, enough for the
  interviewer to follow what follows.
- **Task** — what was your specific responsibility or goal in that
  situation? This clarifies what "you" actually did versus what the team
  or others did.
- **Action** — the specific steps *you* took. This is the part that
  deserves the most detail — vague answers ("we worked hard and fixed
  it") are far weaker than specific ones ("I identified that the slowdown
  was in the database query, added an index on the lookup column, and
  reduced the query time from 2s to 80ms").
- **Result** — what happened, ideally with a concrete outcome or number.
  If there's a lesson learned or something that would be done differently
  next time, mentioning it shows reflection, which interviewers value.

**Common mistakes when using STAR:**
- Spending too long on Situation/Task and rushing the Action — the Action
  is almost always what the interviewer actually wants to hear, since
  it's what demonstrates the candidate's actual skill and judgment.
- Using "we" throughout instead of "I" — even in team projects, the
  interviewer needs to know specifically what the candidate personally
  did, not just what the team accomplished together.
- Not having a real result — a story without any outcome, even a modest
  one, is hard for an interviewer to evaluate; if the result is still in
  progress, say so and explain what's expected.

**For technical explanation questions** (not behavioral ones): structure
the answer top-down — state the core idea or answer first in one
sentence, then explain the reasoning or details, rather than building up
to the answer at the end. This respects the interviewer's time and makes
it easy to follow even if they only catch the first sentence.
